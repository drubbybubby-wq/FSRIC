Built for teams doing real research, not science-fair posters.

FSRIC asks student teams to identify an unsolved regional or global problem, build a working prototype or model, and defend it in front of a panel that has actually shipped the thing they're being asked about.

Problem-first, not project-first

Teams start from a documented problem statement, not a pre-picked science fair topic. The proposal round exists to test whether the problem is real before any building starts.

Practitioner judges

Judging panels are staffed by working engineers, researchers, and clinicians in the relevant field for each track, not generalist volunteers.

Built to continue past the trophy

Finalist teams get six months of mentor office hours and provisional patent filing support if their prototype is novel enough to warrant it.

Four tracks. One shared bar for rigor.

Every track is judged against the same four criteria below — the tracks split by subject matter, not by difficulty.

Sustainable Energy & Environment
Prototypes and models addressing regional energy, water, waste, or climate resilience problems — from grid-scale ideas down to household hardware.
last year: 34 teams
Biomedical & Health Technology
Diagnostics, assistive devices, and health-access tools. Teams working with biological samples must submit a lab safety plan at proposal stage.
last year: 28 teams
Applied Physics & Engineering
Mechanical, robotic, and materials-science builds solving a defined physical constraint — load, distance, precision, durability, or cost.
last year: 22 teams
Computational & Data Science
Models, simulations, and data tools with a real dataset and a stated real-world decision the model is meant to inform.
last year: 19 teams

Five stages, October to March.

Every stage has a hard deadline. Extensions aren't granted — teams that need more time should register for the following season instead.

Oct 3

Registration opens

Teams register with a working title and track selection. Advisor sign-off is required for teams under 18.

open now
Nov 14

Problem statement & proposal due

A 3-page proposal defining the problem, prior attempts at solving it, and the team's proposed approach. Reviewed by track judges before any building starts.

Dec 5

Proposal decisions released

Advancing teams get written judge feedback and a build budget stipend, mailed as a reimbursable grant for parts and materials.

Feb 6–7

Regional semifinals

In-person prototype demos and a 12-minute panel defense at one of four regional host campuses. Remote demo slots available for teams over 200 miles from a host site.

Mar 21

Final showcase & awards

Twelve finalist teams present to the full judging panel and a public audience. Awards, mentorship placements, and patent-support offers are announced same day.

Every round, the same four-part rubric.

Judges score independently and reconcile disagreements over 15 points as a panel, in front of the team, before releasing a final score.

35%

Problem validity

Is the problem real, documented, and still unsolved at the scale the team is targeting?

30%

Technical execution

Does the prototype or model actually do what the team claims, under questioning?

20%

Feasibility beyond the demo

Cost, materials, and manufacturability if this had to leave the lab bench.

15%

Defense under panel

How the team responds to a judge who disagrees with a design choice, live.

Common questions from advisors.

Who can enter?

Any student in grades 9–12 attending a school within the participating region, on a team of one to four students. Teams may be single-school or mixed across schools, but need one signed-off adult advisor.

Do we need a finished prototype to register?

No. Registration only requires a track selection and working title. The prototype itself is built between the proposal decision in December and the semifinal in February.

Is there an entry fee?

No entry fee. Teams that advance past the proposal round receive a materials stipend rather than paying one.

Can a team change tracks after registering?

Only up until the proposal deadline. After proposals are submitted, a team's track is locked for the rest of the season.

What happens if a team can't attend semifinals in person?

Teams more than 200 miles from a regional host site can request a remote demo slot when they register. This must be requested before the proposal deadline, not after.

Are prior competition entries eligible?

A project previously entered in another regional or national competition can be entered here only if it's substantially extended with new work done this season.
